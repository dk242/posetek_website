# PoseTek Draft B: creative and production plan

Prepared September 21, 2026. Audience confirmed by Dylan: **club directors and coaches**.
This is a proposed creative direction and storyboard, not an approved final edit.

## Recommendation

Build the film around **“See the team. Understand each player.”** The central question
is **“What should your team train next?”** Show PoseTek helping a coach move from
testing evidence to an individual training focus, with players training between
assessments and coaches reviewing what changes.

Retain Draft B's useful Test → Understand → Train → Retest structure. Give it the
current website's dark green identity, fine pose landmarks, athletic typography,
and a clear club-to-player story. Let the pose, one result, and one drill each own
the frame at different moments.

Working format: 45 seconds, vertical 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. Optional follow-on
deliverables are a recomposed 1920 × 1080 version for pitch presentations/email
and a 15-second social cut. None has been rendered yet.

## Look and feel

Use the source website as the visual authority, with external references informing
motion and framing rather than changing PoseTek's identity.

| Element | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Main canvas | Deep green `#04130e`; panels `#0a211a`. |
| Type and supporting detail | Off-white `#f0f5ed`; muted green `#a9bdb1`; dividers `#254036`. |
| Focus and action | Marketing lime `#b7f34a`. Preserve native UI colors inside faithful product views. |
| Headings | Barlow Condensed, bold uppercase; short phrases with generous scale. |
| Body / results | Inter for readable copy; IBM Plex Mono for labels and units. |
| Brand | Current lime-square P and POSETEK header treatment. |
| Pose figures | Current thin lime/mint skeleton connections and 33 landmarks, ball and floor shadow where present. Avoid adding a thick glow or reviving the historical body mesh. |
| Composition | One dominant subject occupying roughly 60–75% of the usable frame; no full desktop dashboard reduced to phone size. |

Keep human movement recognizable. Camera motion may reveal the existing 3D pose;
it must not manufacture a new measured pose. The hero's depth is estimated from
recorded landmarks, not a calibrated volumetric body scan. Use a restrained camera
orbit around a held pose, then cut to actual recorded playback where movement is
needed. Never blend different athletes or unrelated repetitions into a measurement.

Use a recurring lime selection marker to connect the story: movement detail →
player focus → drill → comparison. This is an editorial guide, not an animation
claiming that a particular landmark mathematically becomes a score.

Three transition families are enough:

1. **Matched position and scale:** a selected player card expands into the profile;
   the selected drill expands into the phone demonstration.
2. **Masked reveal:** push into one result or cue while adjacent detail falls away.
3. **Movement cut:** cut on a foot plant or ball touch when changing from analysis
   to training footage.

Use approximately 0.35–0.55 seconds for panel transitions, eased arrivals and
departures, and at least two seconds of stable reading time for each essential
result. Overlap movement slightly where it helps continuity. Keep figures and
numbers still while they need to be read. These timings are creative starting
points to test in the animatic, not requirements of a particular library.

## Proposed narration and sound

Approximately 90 words, leaving room for pauses and the end card:

> What should your team train next? Start with evidence. PoseTek brings six
> performance tests, movement analysis, and player profiles into one connected
> system. See your club, understand each player, and turn individual priorities
> into focused training. Players take guided sessions into the mobile app.
> Coaches can review their progress. Then retest, review what changed, and choose
> the next step. From the whole club to each player, PoseTek supports development
> between tests. Let's plan your team's next step. Talk about your team at
> posetek.net/coaches.

Use a restrained athletic percussion track, initially around 100–110 BPM, with
ball touches and footsteps supporting selected cuts. Keep narration clear; avoid
large sound effects on every transition. Provide captions and ensure the story
still works muted. Record a timing read before locking the script. Music choice
and the final voice are production decisions still to be made.

## Production approach

**Recommended:** Remotion with PoseTek's existing source assets and Three.js pose
geometry, starting with a short proof render. This keeps text, UI, data, camera
positions, timing and alternate aspect ratios editable and repeatable.

[Remotion's official agent skills](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills) support
agent-assisted video work. Its [ThreeCanvas integration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three-canvas)
supports React Three Fiber; [GSAP integration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gsap)
can synchronize timelines to video frames. The existing PoseTek renderer uses
Svelte/Threlte, so it is not a direct React import: either port the small rendering
layer while preserving geometry/materials, or capture a controlled version of the
existing component. Confirm the better route with a 6–8 second proof before building
the full timeline. No packages were installed for this planning work.

Higgsfield is available in the plugin catalog but has no callable connected tools
in this session. Its public [AI Motion Designer](https://higgsfield.ai/ai-motion-designer)
and [After Effects workflow article](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/ai-motion-designer-after-effects-gpt)
describe native After Effects editing. Its [Marketing Studio help article](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/tools/how-do-i-use-marketing-studio-to-create-video-ads)
and newer landing pages describe different integration capabilities, so verify the
connected plugin's actual tools before assigning production steps to it.

Public workflow documentation does not establish Higgsfield's private internal
library stack. Remotion is our proposed production method, not a claim about what
Higgsfield uses internally. Once connected, Higgsfield could help explore an intro
or editorial transition; core PoseTek measurements, pose geometry and product screens
should remain grounded in verified source.

## Production sequence and acceptance

1. Build three style frames: the opening pose, the selected player/focus, and the
   retest comparison. Resolve composition and phone-size legibility here.
2. Render a 6–8 second motion proof joining pose → one source-grounded callout →
   profile. Check thin lines, camera behavior, transitions, text and footage quality.
3. Assemble the 45-second animatic with a timing read and music direction. Confirm
   the coach story is understandable without sound before detailed finishing.
4. Finish the master with final narration, licensed music and captions; then
   recompose the horizontal version and short cut if those deliverables are wanted.
5. Review at actual phone size and full resolution. Check sample continuity,
   unchanged metrics/units, honest comparison scale, caption clearance, contrast,
   readable end card, clean pose lines and audio balance.

Any additional references or skills supplied by Dylan can refine the style frames
before the edit is built. The specific voice, music and final distribution formats
remain open production choices. Do not add unsupported promises of improvement,
injury prediction, real-time coach monitoring or a specific training outcome.
